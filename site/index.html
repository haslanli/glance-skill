Why the October AWS bill jumped

The bill went from $2,110 in September to $4,760 in October (+$2,650, about 2.25×). Two Terraform changes account for about 95% of the increase. Both are inferences from lining up the cost data with the changelog dates; I haven't checked them against traffic logs.

Service Sep Oct Change
NAT Gateway $110 $2,240 +$2,130
CloudWatch $70 $450 +$380
Other $30 $125 +$95
EC2 / RDS / S3 $1,900 $1,945 +$45

1. NAT Gateway (+$2,130): ECR VPC endpoints removed on Oct 6 (#412)

  • Data going through the NAT went from 2,400 GB to 49,800 GB (about 21×).
  • At $0.045/GB, 49,800 GB comes to about $2,241, which matches the bill almost exactly. So this is processing charges, not hourly charges.
  • Removing ecr_api and ecr_dkr sends container image pulls through the NAT, where every GB is billed. The endpoints themselves only cost tens of dollars a month, so the change didn't save money overall.
  • It will be worse in November. The change was only live from Oct 6 to Oct 31, so a full month would add roughly $2,500 or more.
  • One thing to check: most of an ECR image download actually comes from S3. If the VPC has an S3 gateway endpoint (a free route to S3 that skips the NAT), most of that traffic shouldn't reach the NAT at all. 47 TB extra suggests there is no S3 gateway endpoint, or something else also started going out through the NAT. To confirm, look at Cost Explorer by day for NatGateway-Bytes (it should jump on Oct 6), or check VPC Flow Logs to see where the traffic is going.

Fix: revert #412, or at least add an S3 gateway endpoint. Also check why images are pulled so often, for example image pull policies or tasks restarting repeatedly.

2. CloudWatch (+$380): log-shipper v2.3 deployed with LOG_LEVEL=debug on Oct 3

  • 760 GB of logs went into CloudWatch, at $0.50/GB that's $380, exactly the increase.
  • Log groups are set to "Never expire", so those 760 GB, and everything logged from now on, will be stored and billed every month (about $0.03/GB-month) and that charge keeps growing.

Fix: set LOG_LEVEL back to info and give the log groups a retention period (for example 30 days).

Caveats

  • "Other" (+$95): the data doesn't show what this is. Break it down by service or usage type in Cost Explorer.
  • Not final: the CSV notes that Oct 28–31 are estimated.

Fixing both issues should bring the bill back to roughly $2,100–2,200 a month.

Why the AWS bill jumped in October

Short version: October came to $4,760, up from $2,110 in September. Almost all of the extra $2,650 is NAT Gateway and CloudWatch, and both trace back to two changes made in the first week of October.

Monthly totals

May $1,840 · June $1,910 · July $1,880 · August $2,020 · September $2,110 · October $4,760

September → October by service

Service September October Change
NAT Gateway $110 $2,240 +$2,130
CloudWatch $70 $450 +$380
EC2 $1,180 $1,210 +$30
RDS $430 $435 +$5
S3 $290 $300 +$10
Other $30 $125 +$95

What happened

  1. Debug logging left on. log-shipper v2.3, deployed Oct 3, runs with LOG_LEVEL=debug. Its output leaves the private subnets through the NAT Gateway: about 38 TB in October. The same logs also go to CloudWatch Logs (~760 GB ingested at $0.50/GB ≈ $380).
  2. ECR endpoint deleted. Terraform change #412 (Oct 6) removed the ECR VPC endpoints, so container image pulls now go through the NAT Gateway too: about 9 TB.

NAT data processed went from 2.4 TB to 49.8 TB, at $0.045/GB.

Fixes, in order

  1. Set LOG_LEVEL=info on log-shipper and redeploy. Removes ~38 TB/month of NAT traffic and most of the CloudWatch ingestion.
  2. Restore aws_vpc_endpoint.ecr_api and aws_vpc_endpoint.ecr_dkr in Terraform. Costs about $29/month (2 interface endpoints × 2 AZs) and saves roughly $400/month in NAT processing.
  3. Set CloudWatch log retention to 14 days (currently "Never expire").
  4. Add a billing alarm at $2,500.

If 1 and 2 are done by Nov 5, November should land around $2,150.

Not verified

  • Cost Explorer figures for Oct 28–31 are still estimates.
  • I didn't check Savings Plan coverage for EC2.
  • The "Other" increase (+$95) isn't explained yet.

Safari: 3 failures

Firefox: 2 failures

  1. Fix requestIdleCallback in AddressSuggestions.tsx before Thursday's release.

  4. In autocomplete, wait for the suggestions request instead of a fixed 250 ms. Give live-updates a longer timeout.

Why not just use dataviz?

dataviz draws the chart you ask for. glance re-tells an answer you already have.

  • It decides what's worth a picture. Whether anything in the answer is, which part, and which form shows it best. Then it rewrites the words around it.
  • It keeps every fact and invents nothing. Every number, name, path, command, warning and open question stays in. No trend lines, averages or projections; a missing value shows as —.
  • The answer comes first. One plain sentence, then the picture, then a few bullets. You have the point before you read a single axis.
  • It only draws when there's data. Reasoning stays as words, so you never get a chart for the sake of a chart.
  • Every picture comes from one tested kit, checked in light mode, dark mode and narrow side panels. Its thin marks, one-axis rule and color checks come from dataviz's own rules.

Use dataviz when you want Claude to make a chart. Use glance when Claude has already answered and you want to understand it fast.

Readable by everyone

About one man in twelve can't easily tell red from green. Most charts lean on exactly that pair, so glance is built to work without it.

  • Blue says look here, red says negative. Red appears only on a value past a limit, target or budget, or on something failing, and never alone: the value also runs past the tick on its gauge, or says "fail".
  • No green, no amber. An improvement stays soft blue. Amber and the alert red differ by only 3.2 for red-green color-blind readers, where 8 is the minimum.
  • Every value is printed, and each picture opens with a one-sentence summary for screen readers.
